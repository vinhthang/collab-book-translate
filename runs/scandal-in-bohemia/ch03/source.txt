# Chapter III
</original>
</segment>

<segment id="S-0002" type="paragraph">
<original>
The alarm of fire was admirably done. No sooner had the cry been raised and the thick cloud of smoke billowed through the sitting-room window of Briony Lodge than we saw Irene Adler dart automatically toward a recessed sliding panel beside the mantelpiece to rescue what she valued most. Satisfied that the hiding-place of the photograph was betrayed, Holmes slipped out of the house, shedding his clerical disguise as we walked back toward Baker Street.
</original>
</segment>

<segment id="S-0003" type="paragraph">
<original>
Yet as we turned into our own doorway at 221B Baker Street, a slim youth in an ulster coat hurried past us along the pavement and called out in a clear, musical voice: "Good-night, Mister Sherlock Holmes." Holmes started and stared back into the lamp-lit fog. "I've heard that voice before," he muttered thoughtfully. "Now, I wonder who the deuce that could have been?"
</original>
</segment>

<segment id="S-0004" type="paragraph">
<original>
When His Majesty the King of Bohemia accompanied us to Briony Lodge the following morning, the bird had flown. In the secret recess behind the sliding panel lay not the cabinet photograph of the two together, but a portrait of Irene Adler alone in evening dress, accompanied by a letter addressed to Sherlock Holmes in which she revealed that she had penetrated his disguise and had departed England forever with her husband, promising never to use the photograph against the King. And that was how a great scandal threatened to affect the kingdom of Bohemia, and how the best plans of Mr. Sherlock Holmes were beaten by a woman's wit.
</original>
